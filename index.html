Sustainable Tiny Home

A tiny home is a very small, compact, self-contained dwelling often ranging from 10 to 50 square metres.

My Client

My tiny home is designed for a retired gardener, Uncle Ray, to live independently in a warm country town in Queensland. This client needs a safe, affordable, eco-friendly, and accessible home. They need somewhere to live, sleep, relax, tend to their gardens, cook, and store their belongings. The tiny home is situated near a community garden, giving Uncle Ray an opportunity to nuture his gardening hobby.

Challenges

One challenge is that the space allocated is very small, and a wheelchair-bound person can get around easily without any tight corners or small gaps. My design helps by having large doorways, wide corridors, built-in storage, and a simple square layout that has a L-shaped shipping container plus a 2.5m x 2m garden to fulfill Uncle Ray's gardening hobbies.

Figure 1: Architectural 20.68 m² studio floor plan layout optimizing accessibility and workflow.

My Design

My tiny home is made from a reused 20ft shipping container, plus an 8ft for the shorter L-shaped section. Because the container is almost square, including the place where Uncle Ray can grow his plants in the vertical garden. Where possible, I added storage and used some unconventional techniques to maximise storage and living area while minimising space. One example of this is a sofa bed, a classic choice. By day, a comfy spot to relax. By night, it pulls out into a bed. On the floorplan, I have made it into a bed as Uncle Ray already might have a wheelchair to sit in. Also not shown on the floorplan, above the stovce in the kitchen, there are hanging pegs to put pots, pans, cutlery etc when not in use. This saves space because otherwise, I would have to make a seperate area to put all the utensils. By eliminating the shower lip entirely, the shower serves as a wheelchair turning zone when not in use, given the narrow walkways that are wide enough to accommodate wheelchair one-way but might not be enough to allow a wheelchair to turn. Finally, a shelf that is 114.3 cm (45in) high allows stuff to be easily reached from a wheelchair. This keeps stuff off the counter while keeping them in easy arms-length reach. The kitchen includes a 4-burner cooktop, a pantry, small oven, dishwasher, fridge, washing machine, and high and low cabinets. There are wide windows with shade to cover at night and let in natural light by day.

Spatial Optimization & Layout Improvements

While maintaining the strict 20.68 m² interior footprint, the floor plan flow can be advanced with these key accessibility improvements:

  • Universal Turning Radius: Reorienting the kitchen counter layout expands the entry core, offering a completely open central turning circle for a walker or wheelchair. This ensures Uncle Ray can navigate the primary food preparation zones safely and without tight constraints.
  • Curbless Wet Room Bathroom: Transitioning the enclosed bathroom design into a step-free wet room removes tripping thresholds and guarantees effortless transitions. It features integrated grab rails and a wide pocket sliding door for smooth lateral clearance.
  • Dual-Side Bed Clearance: Shifting the mattress layout slightly off the corner walls preserves clear pathways on both sides. This optimization makes routine tasks like changing linen straightforward and leaves ample space for assistive support frames if needed.
  • Ergonomic Low-Counter Reach: Lowering portions of the living and storage shelves down to a maximum height of 120 cm places daily essentials within arm's reach, reducing back strain and fall hazards for independent elderly living.

Sustainability & Resource Management

The tiny home integrates environmentally conscious active and passive infrastructure to ensure self-sufficiency in a warm country climate:

  • Closed-Loop Waste System: A specialized composting toilet breaks down organic matter safely, eliminating heavy blackwater sewage plumbing and generating rich, chemical-free soil fertilizer. This organic byproduct directly nourishes Uncle Ray's food crops and vertical garden layers.
  • Rainwater Harvesting Matrix: Structural roof drainage channels runoff straight into an outdoor storage tank, securing independent off-grid irrigation for the gardens. This closed collection process buffers seasonal regional droughts and significantly cuts down utility water bills.
  • Passive Thermal Mass Envelope: Wall insulation makes sure that Uncle Ray's tiny home does not vary too wildly in cold and warm, keeping in warm air in winter and keeping in cold air in summer.
  • Electricity Generating Solar Panels Sustainably generating electricity, while not using fossil fuels in the process. With a solar battery, it stores electricity to use at night for lights.
  • Sustainable Grey-water SystemWastewater from showers and sinks go underground into a tank, reused by watering Uncle Ray's crops. It means that no water gets wasted, so that Uncle Ray doesn't have to pay as much for water.

Materials & DIY Construction

To bring the architectural floor plan to life, a physical scale model will be constructed using the following DIY craft materials:

  • Thick Corrugated Cardboard: Used to construct structural outer walls and the foundation base slab, cut cleanly according to your chosen scale dimensions.
  • Paddle-Pop Sticks: Laid down side-by-side to fabricate Uncle Ray's timber shaded deck, external seating, and custom herb planters.
  • High-Temp Glue Gun: Used for fast, rigid bonding across complex structural wall intersections and fragile miniature furniture blocks.
  • Recycled Objects & Media: Cylindrical plastic caps represent the rainwater harvesting tank, while wooden matches construct the vertical garden frame posts.
